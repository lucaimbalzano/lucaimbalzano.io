In some ways, we live in a very strange era: we are at a point where the growth curve of artificial intelligence is still in an early phase. Before artificial intelligence gets the chance to evolve by itself, in cycles; before we are overwhelmed by constant novelty, or by ambiguities we cannot investigate.

And yet, if I stayed in a room of my apartment, disconnected from the world, I probably wouldn't notice.
I wouldn't notice wars, I wouldn't notice earthquakes, I wouldn't notice…

As if, at times, the noise we hear were the silent noise of the devices we interact with.
Every time I stay away from a device for at least 72 hours, I feel my mind waking up from an imperceptible, invisible anesthesia.

In the first industrial revolution, in Great Britain, with coal we created a new kind of society, handing authority to the great industries without really understanding why. A huge leap forward, but also a general compression of creativity, traded for time.
An entrepreneur is creative by nature, and the first revolution left many of them out.

Today we are living through a necessary change, one that in 2100 we will probably look back on with relief, and partly with regret.
We are witnessing the birth of new tools that can help us, accelerating creativity and the production of goods and services, but also centralizing a government that, in practice, will never be able to bring justice to the grey areas.

The world cannot be governed, and government will always be an attempt to do so.

After all, I am in favour of innovation and technology, but I know that above all I seek treasures that are not of this world, and a justice that is not of this world.
